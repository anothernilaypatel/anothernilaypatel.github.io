Ranked top 1 on a monthly Super Auto Pets leaderboard.

Versus keeps one score for matchmaking and for the leaderboard. The [ranked mode](https://superautopets.wiki.gg/wiki/Version_0.28) is an ELO rating, and a game moves that rating from the opponent's score. This problem takes the queue as given. A game is offered only inside a 250-point gap, and most games are dealt inside 100. A win can be held and cashed in later, and the points use the two ratings at the cash-in. The game in the example is dealt with both players at 1,500. You climb to 1,700 before cashing the win in, and the opponent is still at 1,500. Find what that win is worth.

The expected score of the player ahead by $d$ points is

$$
E(d) = \frac{1}{1 + 10^{-d/400}}.
$$

A result $S$ then moves the rating by

$$
\Delta = K \, (S - E(d)).
$$

$S$ is 1 for a win and 0 for a loss, and $K$ sets the size of the update. The gap at cash-in is $d = 200$, so

$$
E(200) = \frac{1}{1 + 10^{-1/2}},
$$

which is about 0.76. That win was the expected result about 76% of the time, and the surprise in it is about 0.24. The example prices the cashed-in win at 5 points, so $0.24K = 5$ and $K$ is about 20.81. An even game then pays about 10.41 for a win.

The same $K$ prices the rest of the window. A gap of 100 is the edge of the band where most games are dealt. A gap of 250 is the edge of the band where a game can still be offered. The 200-point row is the held win. The percentages and the points are rounded to two decimals.

A loss cashed in at the same 200-point gap would cost about 15.81 points. The update pays the favorite only the part of the win that was not already expected, and it charges the favorite the part that was. Holding the win is what opens the gap. The queue paired two players at 1,500, inside both bands. At 1,700, a player still sitting at 1,500 is 200 points away, inside the 250 cap and outside the 100 band where most games are dealt. The 5 points are the price of that pairing after the ratings have moved apart. Ten held wins are 50 points, stacked on the climb from 1,500 to 1,700, and each one is priced as a favorite's win because those opponents stayed at 1,500.
