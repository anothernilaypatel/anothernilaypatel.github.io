Ranked top 1 on a monthly Super Auto Pets leaderboard.

A versus game is one match. It is queued, it is played, and the rating moves when it ends. I found that asyncs are a different position. An async is another game running at the same time, so several of them are several results in flight from the same stretch of play. That is similar to leverage. The versus game is one unit of the edge. Each async is another unit open beside it.

The queue still has a band. A game is offered only inside 250 ELO, and most games land inside 100. The points wait for the cash-in. I found that a win can be held, and that a win will not cash in for less than 5. The ratings that count are the ratings at that cash-in.

The [ranked mode](https://superautopets.wiki.gg/wiki/Version_0.28) is an ELO rating. The expected score of the player ahead by $d$ points is

$$
E(d) = \frac{1}{1 + 10^{-d/400}}.
$$

An unconstrained win then moves the rating by $K(1 - E(d))$, where $K$ is the size of an update. The cash-in pays that amount, and it pays 5 when the formula would pay less. The surprise in a win is $1 - E(d)$. A coin flip, a gap of 0, has a surprise of $1/2$. The surprises below are rounded to two decimals.

The late cash-in uses that floor. I play the games while both players are at 1,500, inside the band, and I leave the wins uncashed. The climb to 1,700 comes from the games that settle. The opponents on the held games can still be at 1,500, so the gap at cash-in is 200. That gap is outside the 100-point band where most games are dealt, and it is still inside the 250 cap. The higher rating was expected to win about 76% of those, so the surprise is about 0.24, about half of a coin flip. The formula's payout shrinks with that surprise. The minimum cash-in is 5, so each held win still pays 5.

Each async is its own cash-in. Ten of them are 50 points, taken after the climb, from opponents who stayed at 1,500. The leverage is having those games open together. The floor is what keeps the late ones worth cashing.
