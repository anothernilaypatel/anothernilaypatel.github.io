It was an ordinary October day while I was walking through my university campus when I saw a flyer for a hackathon they were hosting (a startup weekend). There was an admission fee for other people, but since Wentworth was hosting it, Wentworth students were able to attend for free. Despite this being a full-weekend commitment, I signed up without much thought.

After some idea generation and initial pitching, teams started to form. I ended up joining a team of 11 with the idea to build a B2C tourism application for when companies go on business trips to other areas. The idea was to have a decentralized map in which locals can upload their favorite parts of the city so that newcomers can have accurate/realistic guides rather than defaulting to unformulated Google responses or TripAdvisor, which isn't curated by locals. The unique thing about this team was that we spanned multiple ages, from high schoolers to PhDs, and multiple careers, from an artist to a bunch of computer scientists. Throughout this weekend, there was a ton of business and development being created. Some of the team even ended up taking a 45-minute tram to Faneuil Hall to ask tourists what they wanted in a tourism app. I focused on building an accurate geospatially-aware map and building a video montage maker for the team.

After the gruesome weekend came the final pitching. We ended up placing 4th. However, with a team dropping out and a team that was found to be cheating, we ended up placing 2nd. Although the pitches are no longer on YouTube, the only remaining artifact I was able to find was [this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8Kd95VEywaU). Part of the prize was a studio space to work in for the season, but with all of us having completely different backgrounds, it was hard for it to be sustainable since most of us still had degrees to finish. The cliché takeaway was that you can build anything with anyone, but the more realistic takeaway was that the most important thing to a competition or an exam is optimizing for the target audience. The best instance of this was when we were naming our team. We started off with the name "My Little Adventure", but in conversations with the judges, most of them preferred the name "My Not So Little Adventure" since the original name could prevent future growth. Although I disagree with it, since it becomes a mouthful to say, I was not the target audience: the judges were.
