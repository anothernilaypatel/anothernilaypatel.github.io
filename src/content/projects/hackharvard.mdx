If you notice, there is no placement for the HackHarvard hackathon. To be direct, I was never a part of it. A more realistic phrase might be that I shouldn't have been a part of it.

It starts off with my friend messaging me while I was still in university at Wentworth. He mentioned that he was coming to HackHarvard for the weekend, and he wanted to hang out with me before the hackathon started. With only a few hours of notice, I decided that I should go see him and embarked on a 45-minute walk to Harvard's campus. I ended up fraternizing with his hackathon group, which was primarily his university friends. As the startup was ready to take place, I was getting ready to head out, but I made a shocking observation. There was no way to check to see if people were eligible to even compete at this hackathon. Although my name would not be on any of the material, there was nothing stopping me from consuming the snacks and providing help to my new friends in their journey through this hackathon. Thus, I took the role of a PM. I was allocating work, sleep schedules, and even energy drinks. The most impact I had on this was helping break down how to create a seamless file-in, file-out system. The project was to have a third-party system audit and correct doctor-patient visits. The file would be a history/record of family history and previous visits. This circular tool was a remarkable idea that came to me at 1 a.m., when two of our backend programmers were already asleep. Again, I couldn't make any code edits, since they would save to the repository, so I picked up a whiteboard and a marker, and I taught the frontend engineer how to do backend work, specifically for this problem.

Although these actions may not have been well advised, the law is only as good as its enforcement. I cannot claim any of the successes that this team had, since I was never truly a part of it.

The icing on the top is that this team lost some points since some judges feared that it could violate HIPAA. We weren't the most knowledgeable on policy, and our judge worked in the healthcare sector, so we assumed that she knew what she was talking about, and by no means do I hold resentment for that. The funny coincidence is that [DocScribe](https://www.docscribe.health/en) is now a real thing, in which they have a branch that does exactly what our project was aiming to do, or at least something similar to it. Who truly knows if they "got inspiration" from what we did, but at least my friends and I can have a good laugh about it.
