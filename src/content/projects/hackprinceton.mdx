HackPrinceton fell during exam season, but my friends from HackHarvard were flying in from across the country to compete, so I wrapped up my exams and joined them, this time as a registered competitor. HackPrinceton leaned heavily toward the intersection of finance and computer science, with a number of hedge funds sponsoring the event and running tracks of their own.

We considered several ideas over the weekend, from turning predictive market indicators into a poker-style game for the quantitative finance tracks to a tool for sending stablecoin payments to people who had just been through a natural disaster. We chose the second because it let us use the widest range of tools in creative ways, and we entered it in Coinbase's Best Use of OnChainKit track. A specialized track meant a smaller field with a high technical bar, and it gave us more time for direct conversations with the judges.

The result was ReliefChain. People and organizations send money through Solidity smart contracts, and recipients can access the funds only after agreeing to terms set by the giver, much like a short terms of service attached to each payment. That lets donors make sure, for example, that relief money isn't used to rebuild in the same flood zone. And because the money moves directly from device to device, it skips the transport and intermediaries that slow traditional aid down.


We placed 2nd in Coinbase's Best Use of OnChainKit track.

One of my most memorable moments came outside the build. HackPrinceton offered a [schedule of speakers](https://docs.google.com/document/d/111nivsg1wPCAeQJqp7DcvVR-sw8lW3H2g904TjF6mFA/edit?usp=sharing), and I made it a goal to attend every talk on top of programming. The one that stuck with me most was Batu Arisoy's lecture on optimizing large language models. I took notes as if it were a class, working through how LLMs process massive prompts through tokenization, encodings, and embeddings, and my phone is still full of photos of his blackboards and slides. I can't claim to know nearly as much as he does, but the talk showed me what it looks like to build a career around deep specialization, and it shaped how I think about both the topic and my own path.
